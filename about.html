01 / The premise

Before the internet, there was the shop.

A single-player card-shop adventure about collecting, trading, and learning the strange little rituals that made the era unforgettable.

The World

In 1993, the card shop is the center of the community. It's where news spreads, prices are set, decks are built, and countless players' stories begin with a few wins and lucky pack pulls. Heading to the local convention center for a tournament meant more new cards, more new friends, and more opportunities to trade cards, stories, and memories. You played with your siblings, school friends, and anybody willing to sit opposite you.

The Routine

Grow up alongside Magic in the card shop. Collect every iconic set, chase after legendary deck recipes, hunt down the mythical power 9, and collect the books, magazines, and oddities that people would kill for today. Build your reputation, conquer your peers, and challenge iconic foes from Magic's history. When you're done, take over as the shop owner — keep the shelves stocked, order new product, and, of course, spoil Molly!

The Chase

Every pack opening is made to feel as authentic as possible, with period accurate card contents, odds, and randomized box logic applied to every pack and deck in the game, to ensure that the experience captures the true essence of the era. With nearly 500 deck templates to discover and complete, the chase is as legendary as the cards themselves. Play classic tournament winning decks, edition-specific theme decks, legendary meta decks, and more!

The Vibes

'90s Magic is about atmosphere as much as mechanics: loud tables, bright shelves, old cardboard, and the joy of making a deck that feels like it belongs to you. Deck your character out in the styles and colors that define the era. Explore the early days of computers, video games, and the internet as you build your collection. Deal with all of the same limitations and opportunities that defined growing up during the golden age of Magic, and become a local legend.

About the Game

01 / The shop

It's 1993, and the whole world is one card shop: glass cabinets of singles along the wall, a bookshelf full of reference material, guides, and graphic novels, racks full of magazines and comics, a computer station in the corner, and a carpet that has never heard of good taste. Seven cabinets hold sixty singles each, one for every year of the game, unlocking as you advance through the years.

Behind the counter, the shop owner keeps an eye on things, announces when new stock arrives, lets you know about upcoming events, and notices when you start making a name for yourself. Regulars wander in to browse, buy, sit down for a match, and socialize. And Molly, the shop dog, goes wherever she likes.

02 / The player

You are a typical suburban '90s kid with a modest allowance and time to kill. Visit the shop with your best friend and discover the world of Magic with the aid of the benevolent Time Wizard. Pick your perfect '90s outfit and hair style, spend your allowance on packs, decks, singles, books, and magazines, and challenge anyone sitting at a table.

There are two ways to play. In Campaign mode, win matches to build reputation, challenge increasingly difficult opponents and boss characters, play in period-accurate tournaments, discover the history of Magic, and conquer epic challenges to become the greatest Magic player in the world. Beat the campaign to unlock Free Mode, where all previous content is available/repeatable and you can continue building your dream collection.

03 / Collecting

In the '90s, as new sets came out, players had to discover them using different methods. Discover cards in the shop, through playing matches, or in magazines and books. Keep track of fluctuating prices at the shop counter and in magazines like The Duelist and InQuest. The game covers 31 sets and 6,792 cards across seven years of Magic history.

Everything from booster packs to deck display boxes have real-world methodology and odds applied to them, including the distribution of basic lands, commons, uncommons, rares, applied box and pack logic that follows simulated sheet-based card printing, and more.

04 / Deckbuilding

A desk with an old computer is your workbench. Browse your collection, open packs, and build 60-card decks with or without sideboards. Cards are never used up, so the same card can sit in as many decks as you like, as long as you own the card. Create a deck from scratch or use one of the template decks as a starting point.

The real chase is the template decks: 492+ historical decklists from the era, pulled from the 1997 Shandalar video game, deck-building books, InQuest, The Duelist, the 97-99 World Championships, and the 1996 Pro Tour. Collect every card in a list and that deck is yours for good. Then take it to a table and play it against other kids in the shop.

05 / Tournaments & the convention center

Each year the convention center opens its doors for several tournaments; that means more people to play and trade with, unique and rare vendor booths, and more chances to find the cards you could never find at the counter. Rare cards and oddities turn up here more often than at the shop for those that can afford them.

Themed sealed deck tournaments are held throughout the year, offering unique challenges and rewards for those who can master the art of deckbuilding under specific constraints, following the release schedule of each set block. Take your seat, open your packs, build the best deck you can, and see where it takes you. Who knows, maybe you might even take home some prize money!

06 / Time rolls on

The campaign runs from 1993 to 1999 in 26 phases, one for nearly every release from Alpha to Urza's Destiny. Win matches, earn reputation, then go home and sleep on it. When you come back, the shop has new stock on the shelves. Each year ends with a showdown against the shop's current best player.

Guiding you through it is the Time Wizard, a hooded wizard from the future who hands you your first decks, shows up when a new year is ready, and explains the bits of Magic history the game skips over. Beat everything the nineties can throw at you, and the wizard has one final challenge waiting for you...

07 / Prices, cards & decks to discover

Hopping on the internet to check the value of your cards in 1993 wasn't exactly the option that it is today, so kids everywhere had to rely on a few staples of the industry to stay informed. Magazine price guides were the primary source of information, helping players understand the market value of their cards as they fluctuated from month to month. Seeing articles and advertisements in these publications was the best way to stay up-to-date on new set releases, strategies and card synergies, deck recipes and other deckbuilding ideas, upcoming events and tournaments, and changes to the rules or game formats. Several iconic books also provided valuable insights into strategy, collecting, deckbuilding, tournament play, and the history of the game. Buying a periodical in-game unlocks updated price knowledge, card collection data, and deck template recipes to chase as you play through the years.

08 / Under the hood

'90s Magic is a solo project built in Unity 6, modeled in Blender, and coded in VS Code. The card database comes from Scryfall's bulk data, filtered down to the 1993 to 1999 sets, omitting non-English cards, Renaissance, Collector's Edition (1993), Mercadian Masques, Unglued, and the 3 Portal sets. Card art is never bundled with the game: it streams from Scryfall as you play and is cached on your drive. Magazine scans stream the same way from scans on the Internet Archive.

The screens are built with Unity's UI Toolkit, which styles menus much like a web page. The world is dressed with either custom content or modified content from low-poly asset packs, the people are assembled from modular parts, conversations and the shop's ambient chatter run on the Pixel Crushers Dialogue System, and the WoW-style camera is built in Unity with Cinemachine. Even the save files are hand-rolled, so every save slot is its own little world.

About Me

I'm a 42 year old professional graphic designer and art director from Massachusetts. I grew up playing video games since the days of the original Nintendo Entertainment System, and started playing Magic: The Gathering in late 1994. I loved everything from the card artwork to the strategic nature of the game, and was hooked through the rest of the '90s. I still have my old card collection of about 3,000 cards, from Revised Edition to Urza's Destiny, complete with a very well played Underground Sea.

As a professional designer, I'm trying to do as much of the work myself as possible, with project management, planning, research, and occasional implementation (scripting, Blender animation help) help from Claude, and some more occasional research help from ChatGPT/Gemini/Copilot. No artwork, models, or environments are or will be AI generated. All design work is being done manually in Photoshop, InDesign, Figma, and After Effects. All web work is being done manually in HTML, CSS, and JavaScript in Visual Studio Code.

I'm developing this project on my own as a personal passion project and have no association with Wizards of the Coast. As such, this game will likely never be distributed and can not be sold — that was never the point in making it, anyway. This is my first foray into game development and I simply wanted to make a game about a subject I'm passionate about.
